    Approach 1: Recursion Pharmaceuticals Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Analysis

    A Discounted Cash Flow, or DCF, model takes estimates of the cash a company might generate in the future and discounts those amounts back to what they could be worth today. It is essentially asking what a stream of future cash flows might reasonably be worth in the present.

    For Recursion Pharmaceuticals, Simply Wall St uses a 2 Stage Free Cash Flow to Equity model based on cash flow projections in $. The latest twelve month free cash flow is a loss of $387.8 million. Analysts provide detailed estimates for several years, and beyond that point the platform extrapolates additional figures, reaching a projected free cash flow of $176 million in 2030.

    Bringing all of those projected cash flows back to today, the model arrives at an estimated fair value of about $8.70 per share. Compared with the recent share price of around $3.39, this implies the stock trades at a 61.0% discount to that DCF estimate. On this measure, Recursion Pharmaceuticals appears significantly undervalued.

    Approach 2: Recursion Pharmaceuticals Price vs Sales

    For many companies, price based multiples are a quick way to sanity check valuation against what the business is generating today. When a company is profitable, investors often focus on P/E. In cases where earnings are not yet positive, P/S is commonly used instead, as sales can provide a cleaner base to compare across businesses.

    The level of a typical P/S ratio often reflects what the market is expecting for future growth and the risks around achieving it. Higher expected growth and lower perceived risk can support a higher multiple, while slower growth or higher uncertainty tend to line up with a lower multiple.

    Recursion Pharmaceuticals currently trades on a P/S ratio of 23.98x. This sits above the broader Biotechs industry average P/S of 11.01x and above the peer group average of 15.24x. Simply Wall St also calculates a Fair Ratio of 0.00x for Recursion Pharmaceuticals, which is the P/S multiple the model suggests based on factors such as earnings growth, profit margins, industry, market cap and risk profile. Because the Fair Ratio is tailored to the company's own characteristics, it can be more informative than a simple comparison with peers or the overall industry.

    Comparing the current P/S of 23.98x with the Fair Ratio of 0.00x, the shares screen as expensive on this metric.

    Result: OVERVALUED
